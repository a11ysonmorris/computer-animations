Enhancements I made

Enhancement 01

Planet textures

I replaced the default planet materials with higher-resolution textures from solarsystemscope.com, giving each planet realistic surface color and detail instead of flat shading.

Enhancement 02

Sun as the light source

Using Maya's illumination settings, I set the sun as the scene's light source so each planet is lit and shadowed the way it would be from actual sunlight.

Enhancement 03

Planet position and speed

I adjusted the position and orbit speed of each planet so their spacing and motion better reflect their real relative distances and speeds in the solar system.

Modified animation

Playblast v2 — the final animation with all three enhancements applied.

Notes

The textures made the biggest visual difference. By swapping in real planetary surface maps, immediately the scene felt less like a placeholder and more like an actual solar system. The process of switching the light source to the sun took a bit of trial and error in Maya to get the shadows falling naturally on each planet, but it was worth it for how much more physically correct the lighting looks. Finally, adjusting the position and speed of the planets last tied everything together and made the orbits feel more intentional.

Modeling & Shading

Modeling: I built the treasure chest in Maya using polygonal modeling techniques to create the main box, curved lid, and smaller details. I used extrusion and beveling to shape the chest and refine its edges, then adjusted the geometry to create the rounded top and overall proportions. I also modeled the coins, gems, and pearls separately so they could be placed inside the chest.

Shading: I used separate materials for the wooden chest, metal hardware, coins, gems, and pearls to distinguish the different surfaces. I adjusted the materials and lighting to give the objects more depth and detail, and used smooth shading to make the curved parts of the chest and the treasure appear more polished.

Treasure: The treasure was modeled without downloading treasure models from the internet. The final chest contains coins, gems, and pearls.

Project Notes

This project gave me more experience building a complete object from scratch in Maya and thinking about how separate pieces work together as one model. One of the main challenges was creating the curved lid while keeping the shape smooth and consistent with the rest of the chest. I also had to make sure the chest could open correctly and that the treasure stayed inside during the animation. Adding different materials and smooth shading helped give the final model more detail and made the different parts of the chest and treasure easier to distinguish.

Project 3 – Bouncy Ball

Name Allyson Morris
Date 10/14/2026
Project Bouncy Ball

Storyboard & Animation Description

My animation follows a bouncing ball as it moves through the scene. The ball interacts with its surroundings and uses changes in speed, direction, and shape to make the movement feel natural and expressive.

The animation focuses on realistic, physics-based movement while incorporating squash and stretch, timing, and other principles of animation to give the ball personality.

Storyboard 2 — ideation, shot creation, and visual inspiration board. View on Canva

Disney Principles of Animation

The following principles are demonstrated in my animation. Each helps make the ball's movement more believable and visually interesting.

The ball squashing and stretching mid-fall.

Principle 01

Squash and Stretch

The ball changes shape as it hits the ground and rebounds. It squashes on impact and stretches as it moves through the air, emphasizing the force and speed of the movement.

The ball mid-jump, trying to make it over the tree.

Principle 02

Anticipation

Each time the ball jumps at the tree, it builds anticipation because we don't know whether it will make it over. The repeated attempts keep the viewer waiting to see if the next jump will finally work.

The ball sweating and moving slowly before it sees the water.

Principle 03

Timing and Spacing

At the start, the ball moves slowly because it is tired and has no water. Once it spots the puddle, its timing speeds up and it moves faster, jumping with excitement as it tries to reach the water.

Shading & Lighting

I used materials and lighting in Maya to give the ball a more finished appearance and help it stand out from the environment. The shading adds depth to the scene and makes the ball easier to follow throughout the animation.

Render showing the ball's shading and lighting.

Project Notes

This project helped me understand how much personality and emotion can come from animating even a simple object. I learned how to use timing, anticipation, and squash and stretch to make movement feel more expressive, rather than just physically accurate. I also explored how to guide the viewer's attention and build toward a payoff through movement and pacing. One of my biggest takeaways was how much small details can change the way an animation feels and how important it is to think about the story behind the movement, not just the movement itself.
